# Kept — Instagram marketing

Reference for the Kept brand on Instagram: naming decisions, profile setup, the
launch-week content plan with paste-ready captions, and the working rules behind
them. Companion to `app-store-metadata.md`.

Handle: **@kept.cards** · Display name: **Kept** · Site: https://kept.cards ·
App Store: https://apps.apple.com/app/id6809942529

---

## 1. Naming and brand

**The brand is "Kept." Use "Kept Cards" nowhere as a name.**

| Context | Use |
|---|---|
| Logo, in-app, conversation, social display name | **Kept** |
| App Store name | **Kept: Greeting Card Archive** (keep as is) |
| Page titles, social bios, ads, link previews | **Kept — Scan and keep your greeting cards** |
| Standalone "Kept Cards" | never |

Why: "Kept" is short, distinctive, and emotionally right (keepsakes, "the cards
you kept"). "Kept Cards" is a compromise that is neither a clean brand nor a real
descriptor. The domain **kept.cards** already gives the "cards" association for
free — lean into it ("find it at kept dot cards").

The descriptor does the *acquisition* work, not the name: "Kept" alone is a
common word nobody searches for. People discover the app by searching "greeting
card scanner" or "save birthday cards," so keywords carry discovery and "Kept"
carries recognition. Keep them separate.

**Social handles:** `kept.cards` where dots are allowed (Instagram, TikTok),
`keptcards` elsewhere. Claim the handle on every platform now even if you never
post there, so nobody squats on the name.

## 3. Channel priorities

1. **Instagram** — the one to invest in. Visual, emotional product; audience
   skews to people who keep things. Start here.
2. **Pinterest** — strong second, revisit in a month or two. It is a search
   engine, not a feed: people arrive with intent ("how to store greeting cards").
   Pins keep surfacing for months, so it is the lowest-maintenance channel.
   Repost Instagram Reels/images as pins at almost no extra cost.
3. **TikTok** — optional; cross-post Reels once content is flowing.
4. **Facebook** — a page for the older audience and parent groups; not a content
   priority.
5. **X / LinkedIn** — skip. Wrong audience and tone.

Rule: claim the handle everywhere, post on one or two.

## 4. Launch week — content plan

Four posts plus daily Stories. Consistent, not spammy. Emotion first, product
second.

**Before shooting:** only use cards you are comfortable showing the world. Real
cards carry names and personal messages. Pick ones where sender and recipient
are fine being seen, or crop the names out — the emotion survives a hidden name.

### Post 1 · Launch — "Shoebox → archive"

- **Format:** Reel, 10–15 s (or a 2-slide carousel).
- **Shoot:** the real shoebox or drawer, messy and overflowing → cut to the View
  tab grid organized by year. The contrast is the pitch.
- **Pin this post** to the top of the grid (Instagram allows three pins). It is
  the storefront.

```
Twenty years of birthday cards were living in a shoebox in my closet. The shoebox never got opened.

So I built Kept. Scan a card, keep the message inside, find any of them in seconds.

Keep the handwriting. Lose the shoebox. Free on the App Store, link in bio.
```

### Post 2 · The handwriting

- **Format:** single image or 2-slide carousel (front, then inside).
- **Shoot:** close-up of the scanned *inside* of a card, handwriting in focus.
  The most emotional thing to post.

```
The front of the card is nice. The handwriting inside is the part you'd actually miss.

Kept keeps the front and the inside together, so the note is never lost. Only the clutter is.
```

### Post 3 · Scan in seconds

- **Format:** Reel, under 15 s. This is the "how it works."
- **Shoot:** screen recording or over-the-shoulder: lay the card down, tap Take
  Photos, watch the edges get found and cropped clean. Real time, no cuts.

```
No scanner. No fuss. Photograph the card on the kitchen table and Kept finds the edges and crops it clean.

That's the whole process.
```

### Post 4 · Share with family

- **Format:** single image or short Reel.
- **Shoot:** a card from a grandparent, or the Shared-with screen.

```
The cards from Grandma shouldn't belong to whoever happened to keep the box.

Kept lets you share your collection with the people who'd want to see them. Everyone who loved her gets to keep her handwriting.
```

### Stories (daily, low effort)

One a day: a card being scanned, a "which is your favorite" poll, a peek at the
app. Save the best into a **Highlight named "How it works"** so new visitors see
the demo without scrolling.

### Hashtags

A small relevant set on every post beats 30 random ones:

```
#greetingcards #keepsakes #handwritten #familymemories #memorykeeping #sentimental #birthdaycards #scrapbooking
```

## 5. Mechanics

- **The first line is the hook.** Instagram shows only the first line before
  "more" — every caption above opens on the emotional beat, not the product.
- **Reels vertical (9:16).** Shoot phone-upright so they fill the screen.
- **Caption length:** short. Under ~150 words; the first line does the work.
- **Link in bio, always.** URLs in captions are not tappable.
- Post 1 first, pinned. A profile with a great picture and zero posts looks
  abandoned.

## 6. Ideas for later

- A founder-story post ("why I built Kept") — performs well and builds trust.
- Seasonal hooks: Mother's Day, Father's Day, the holidays, Valentine's — the
  moments people receive and keep cards. Swap the App Store **Promotional Text**
  (editable anytime without a review) to match.
- Pinterest boards: "greeting card storage ideas," "keepsake organization."
- Once there is traction: a Facebook Page (needed for ads), then Meta Verified
  only if impersonation becomes a real problem.
